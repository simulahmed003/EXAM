6. Aircraft simple air-cycle refrigeration: 2020 3(c)

Topic A12 · 13 marks · back to all numericals

A. Problem

In an aircraft refrigeration unit using the air cycle, 50 kg/min of air at 200 cm Hg gauge and 205 °C are bled off the air compressor serving the jet engine. The bled air passes through a heat exchanger leaving at 180 cm Hg gauge and 80 °C. It is then expanded through a small cooling turbine to 20 cm Hg vacuum and −10 °C. The air exhausted out of the plane is at 25 °C. Assume cp = 1.0 kJ/kg K. Determine the cooling capacity in TR and the COP based on input power to the bled-off air.

B. UNDERSTANDHow the cycle/process works

For understanding – do not write

Air is the refrigerant. Hot bled air (205 °C) is cooled in the heat exchanger (80 °C), expanded in the cooling turbine to −10 °C, then sent to the cabin, where it warms up to 25 °C before leaving. That warming in the cabin is the cooling it gives.

The pressures (cm Hg) are not needed, because the temperatures at each point are given. The “input” is the energy carried by the bled air above the exhaust state: m cp(205 − 25).

F. WRITE IN EXAMModel answer

Write this in the exam

Given: m = 50 kg/min, cp = 1.0 kJ/kg K, bleed 205 °C, turbine exit −10 °C, cabin exhaust 25 °C.

Refrigerating effect = m cp(texhaust − tturbine) = 50 × 1.0 × (25 − (−10)) = 1,750 kJ/min

Cooling capacity = 1,750 / 210 = 8.33 TR

Input to bled air = m cp(tbleed − texhaust) = 50 × 1.0 × (205 − 25) = 9,000 kJ/min

COP = refrigerating effect / input = 1,750 / 9,000 = 0.19

Assumption stated: input energy measured from the cabin-exhaust state (25 °C).

G. UNDERSTANDCommon mistakes

For understanding – do not write
  • Using the HX exit (80 °C) in the RE. The cooling happens in the cabin, from −10 to 25 °C.
  • Converting the pressures. Not needed here: all temperatures are given.
  • Low COP is normal for air cycles (about 0.2). Do not “correct” it.
