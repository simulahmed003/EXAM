A8

Fuel cell stack (bipolar plates, series connection); DMFC layer structure

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 13 marks in total

Study these

  • One cell gives ~0.7 V, so cells are stacked in series; bipolar plates connect the anode of one cell to the cathode of the next and distribute gases
  • Other connections: edge collection (planar), tubular SOFC with interconnects
  • DMFC MEA: anode flow plate / diffusion layer / Pt-Ru anode / Nafion membrane / Pt cathode / diffusion layer / cathode plate; methanol + water fed directly

A11

Phosphoric acid fuel cell (PAFC): electrolyte properties and functions, electrodes and catalyst

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 13 marks in total

Study these

  • Electrolyte: ~100 % H3PO4 held in a SiC-PTFE matrix, 150-220 °C; conducts H+
  • Properties: thermal, chemical, electrochemical stability, low volatility above 150 °C, tolerates CO2 and ~1 % CO
  • Electrodes: PTFE-bonded carbon on carbon paper, Pt (or Pt alloy) catalyst on carbon black, ~0.1-0.5 mg/cm2

A2

Crystalline silicon structures (mono-, multi-, amorphous); diffusion and drift device concepts

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 12 marks in total

Study these

  • Monocrystalline (one continuous lattice, Cz/FZ), multicrystalline (many grains, cast), amorphous (no long-range order, a-Si:H)
  • Diffusion device: thick p-n homojunction; minority carriers reach the junction by diffusion (needs long diffusion length: c-Si)
  • Drift device: p-i-n; carriers are swept by the built-in field across the intrinsic absorber (needed when diffusion length is short: a-Si:H)

A9

Role of PTFE in alkaline fuel cells; functions of bipolar plates in PEMFCs

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 12 marks in total

Study these

  • Materials: graphite, coated metals, polymer-carbon composites

Solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC): favourable characteristics, electrolyte types

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 12 marks in total

Study these

  • Favourable: high efficiency (and with gas turbine/CHP), fuel flexible with internal reforming, no precious metals, CO is a fuel, no liquid electrolyte management
  • Electrolytes: YSZ (yttria-stabilised zirconia), ScSZ, doped ceria (GDC/SDC), LSGM (doped lanthanum gallate), also proton-conducting perovskites

A7

Fuel cell: definition, difference from a battery, working principle

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 10 marks in total

Study these

  • Fuel cell = electrochemical device converting chemical energy of a fuel (H2) and oxidant (O2) directly into electricity, as long as fuel is supplied
  • Battery stores its reactants inside and runs down/needs recharging; fuel cell is an energy converter with external fuel
  • H2 → 2H+ + 2e− at anode; ions through electrolyte, electrons through external load; O2 + H+ + e− → H2O at cathode

A6

Multijunction cells: challenges; spectral conversion

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 13 marks in total

Study these

  • Multijunction: stack of cells with decreasing band gaps, each absorbing part of the spectrum (efficiency > 40 % with concentration)
  • Challenges: current matching, lattice matching, tunnel junctions, cost, spectral sensitivity, thermal mismatch
  • Spectral conversion: down-conversion (1 high-energy photon → 2 lower), down-shifting, up-conversion (2 sub-gap photons → 1 usable) to reduce thermalisation and transmission losses

A1

Losses in solar cells; optical losses

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 10 marks in total

Study these

  • Losses: optical (reflection, shading, incomplete absorption), thermalisation and non-absorption (spectral mismatch), recombination, resistive (series and shunt)
  • Optical losses = light that never creates an electron-hole pair: reflected from the surface, blocked by front metal, or passing through without absorption
  • Reduced by anti-reflection coating, surface texturing, thin fingers, back reflectors (light trapping)

B10

SEM: backscattered vs secondary electrons; how SEM gives high resolution and depth of field

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 18 marks in total

Study these

  • SE: low-energy (< 50 eV) electrons knocked out of specimen atoms near the surface (inelastic) - topography, best resolution
  • BSE: beam electrons elastically scattered back (> 50 eV, near beam energy), from deeper - atomic-number (Z) contrast and composition
  • SEM: fine focused beam scanned in a raster; small probe gives resolution (~1-10 nm), small aperture and long working distance give large depth of field (~100× optical microscope)

B5

Sputtering: definition and process

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 9 marks in total

Study these

  • Sputtering = ejection of atoms from a solid target by bombardment with energetic ions (usually Ar+), the ejected atoms condensing on a substrate as a thin film

Geometry and components of an X-ray diffractometer

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 9 marks in total

Study these

B7

Obtaining monochromatic Kα radiation from the X-ray tube spectrum

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 8 marks in total

Study these

  • Tube gives continuous (white) radiation plus characteristic Kα and Kβ lines
  • Filter: a foil whose K absorption edge lies between Kβ and Kα of the target (Ni for Cu, Zr for Mo) absorbs Kβ strongly and passes Kα

B4

Hydrogen storage in carbonaceous materials and CNTs: adsorption mechanism, key parameters, applications

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 20 marks in total

Study these

  • Physisorption: H2 molecules held by weak van der Waals forces on carbon surfaces (~4-6 kJ/mol), significant only at low temperature (77 K) and high pressure; reversible

B1

Electric double-layer capacitor (EDLC): why high capacitance, fundamental principle

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 1 of 1 papers · 12 marks in total

Study these

  • C = εA/d: EDLC has huge area (activated carbon 1000-3000 m2/g) and tiny charge separation d (~0.3-1 nm double layer), so capacitance is thousands of farads
  • Charge is stored electrostatically by ions forming a Helmholtz double layer at each electrode; no reaction, so very fast and long cycle life
  • Two double layers in series: 1/C = 1/C+ + 1/C−; energy E = ½CV2
  1. 2022Q5(a)12 marks

    Why does an Electric Double Layer Capacitor (EDLC) show high capacitance? Describe the fundamental principles of EDLC with necessary figure.

Full answer: ESE 4011 Study Pack, topic B1.
