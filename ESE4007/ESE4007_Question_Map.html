A1

What a smart grid is: definition, conventional grid vs smart grid, architecture and components, key drivers, stakeholders, need for Bangladesh

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 45 marks in total

Study these

  • Smart grid = an electricity network that uses two-way digital communication, sensors and automatic control so that power and information flow both ways between utility and consumer
  • Conventional grid: one-way power, centralised generation, few sensors, manual restoration, passive consumers. Smart grid: two-way, distributed generation, self-healing, active consumers
  • Drivers: rising demand, ageing infrastructure, renewables, reliability, climate targets, customer choice; stakeholders: generators, transmission and distribution utilities, regulators, consumers/prosumers, vendors, researchers

Smart meter, AMR vs AMI, how AMI works, architecture for communication between smart meter and utility server

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 40 marks in total

Study these

  • Smart meter = electronic meter that records energy at short intervals and communicates both ways with the utility
  • AMR (automatic meter reading) = one-way, reads the meter automatically for billing. AMI (advanced metering infrastructure) = two-way system: smart meters + communication network + head-end + MDMS, allows remote connect/disconnect, time-of-use pricing, outage alerts

A9

Load flow in smart grids: classical methods, old vs desired load flow, essential features, why needed and challenges

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 30 marks in total

Study these

  • Load flow finds bus voltages (magnitude and angle) and line flows for given loads and generation; classical methods: Gauss-Seidel, Newton-Raphson, fast decoupled (and DC load flow)
  • Old: deterministic, balanced, one snapshot, central generation, transmission only. Desired: handles DG and two-way flow, unbalanced distribution, uncertainty (probabilistic), real-time with PMU data, fast and robust
  • Challenges: intermittent renewables, high R/X distribution feeders (convergence), unbalanced three-phase, size, real-time speed
  1. 2023Q4(c)10 marks

A4

Distributed generation (DG) and microgrid: definition, advantages, configuration, grid-connected and islanded operation

Must study Very likely · 83%Easy

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 39 marks in total

Study these

  • DG = small generation (a few kW to tens of MW) connected to the distribution network near the load: solar PV, wind, micro-turbines, fuel cells, diesel, CHP
  • Advantages: lower losses, better voltage and reliability, deferred network investment, renewables, CHP efficiency, quick to build
  • Microgrid = a group of DGs, storage and loads with its own controller that can run connected to the grid or alone (islanded) via a static switch at the point of common coupling

A7

Demand side management (DSM): load shifting, valley filling, peak clipping (load-shape curves)

Must study Very likely · 83%Easy

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 27 marks in total

Study these

  • DSM = utility actions that change the amount or timing of customers' electricity use to flatten the load curve
  • Peak clipping: cut the peak (direct load control). Valley filling: add load at night/off-peak (EV charging, storage). Load shifting: move load from peak to off-peak (time-of-use tariff, thermal storage)
  • Also: strategic conservation, strategic load growth, flexible load shape

Active vs passive distribution network; active distribution management scheme (block diagram)

Must study Very likely · 83%Easy

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 25 marks in total

Study these

Smart grid communication: SCADA, HAN/NAN/WAN, and where Bluetooth, ZigBee, WLAN, WPAN and WiMAX are used

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

A10

Performance analysis tools: contingency analysis, security-constrained OPF, DSOPF, static security assessment (SSA), congestion management; GIS

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 40 marks in total

Study these

  • Contingency analysis = "what if" a line/generator trips (N-1): check overloads and voltage limits; SSA uses it to classify the system as secure or insecure
  • OPF = minimise cost subject to power flow and limits; SCOPF adds the constraint that the system stays within limits after each contingency; DSOPF = dynamic stochastic OPF (handles time and uncertainty, adaptive)
  • Congestion = a line reaching its limit, managed by re-dispatch, FACTS, pricing; GIS and Google maps show assets and outages on maps for planning and crews

A8

Phasor measurement unit (PMU) and synchronised phasor measuring system (WAMS)

Must study Very likely · 83%Medium

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 20 marks in total

Study these

  • PMU measures voltage and current phasors (magnitude and angle) and frequency, time-stamped by GPS to 1 μs, 30-60 times per second
  • Inside: anti-aliasing filter → A/D converter (sampling locked to GPS via a phase-locked oscillator) → microprocessor (DFT phasor computation) → communication
  • WAMS: many PMUs → PDC (phasor data concentrator) → super PDC at the control centre → applications (state estimation, stability monitoring, protection)

Interoperability and cyber security: threats, false data injection attacks, mitigation

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 50 marks in total

Study these

B3

Reactive power and voltage control: why the grid needs reactive power, control methods, AVR, SVC, improving Pmax

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 43 marks in total

Study these

  • Reactive power (Q) builds the magnetic fields of motors and transformers and holds up voltage; voltage depends mainly on Q, frequency on P
  • Q control devices: generator excitation (AVR), shunt capacitors and reactors, synchronous condensers, tap-changing transformers, SVC, STATCOM, series capacitors
  • Pmax = EV/X: raise it by higher E (excitation), higher V (voltage support), lower X (parallel lines, series capacitors, bundled conductors, FACTS)

  • Simplest: the idea - add sensing, communication and control to the grid. Complex: making millions of devices, markets and people work together securely in real time

B2

Frequency control: why grid frequency matters, turbine speed governing system, load frequency control loop, AGC

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 48 marks in total

Study these

  • Frequency stays at 50 Hz only when generation = load; a load increase slows the machines and frequency falls
  • Speed governor (fly-ball/electronic) senses speed and opens the steam/water valve: primary control with droop R (frequency falls a little, output rises)

B6

Voltage stability: why instability occurs, preventive control, assessment techniques (P-V, Q-V, VIPI, indices)

Must study Very likely · 100%Medium

Asked in 3 of 3 papers · 42 marks in total

Study these

  • Voltage instability = voltages fall uncontrollably because the network cannot supply the reactive power the loads demand (heavy load, long lines, Q limits of generators, tap-changer action, motor loads)
  • Preventive control: reactive reserves, capacitor/SVC switching, generator voltage set-points, tap-changer blocking, load shedding, re-dispatch
  • Assessment: P-V (nose) curve, Q-V curve, modal/eigenvalue analysis, continuation power flow, voltage stability indices (L-index), VIPI (voltage instability proximity index based on the angle between two load-flow solution vectors)

B1

Buses and bus types, infinite bus, bus admittance matrix Ybus and proof of Ibus = YbusVbus

Must study Very likely · 83%Medium

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 21 marks in total

Study these

  • Bus = a node where lines, generators and loads meet. Three types for load flow: slack (V, δ given), PV/generator (P, |V| given), PQ/load (P, Q given)
  • Infinite bus = a bus whose voltage and frequency stay constant whatever power you inject (huge grid, zero internal impedance, infinite inertia)
  • Ybus: diagonal Yii = sum of all admittances connected to bus i; off-diagonal Yij = −(admittance between i and j); from KCL at each bus you get I = YV

B4

Rotor angle stability: steady-state vs transient, swing equation, small-disturbance solution ωn = √(πf0Ps/H), equal area criterion

Must study Very likely · 83%Hard

Asked in 2 of 3 papers · 40 marks in total

Study these

  • Steady-state stability: survive small, slow changes; transient stability: survive a large sudden disturbance (fault, line trip)
  • Swing equation: (H/πf0) d2δ/dt2 = Pm − Pe; the rotor angle accelerates when mechanical input exceeds electrical output
  • Small disturbance: linearise, Ps = Pmaxcosδ0; oscillation at ωn = √(πf0Ps/H). Equal area criterion: stable if decelerating area A2 can equal accelerating area A1
