A1

Fuel properties: octane, cetane, API gravity, RVP, aniline point, diesel index

Must study Very likely · 100%Easy

Asked in 5 of 5 papers · 62 marks in total

Study these

  • Octane number = % iso-octane in iso-octane + n-heptane blend that knocks like the fuel; cetane number = % n-hexadecane in hexadecane + 1-methylnaphthalene blend with the same ignition delay
  • Higher octane lets an SI engine run higher compression ratio / spark advance without knock, so more power and efficiency
  • API = 141.5/SG − 131.5 (hydrometer); RVP at 38 °C in a 4:1 air chamber (volatility); aniline point (aromatics); Diesel index = aniline point (°F) × API / 100

Need for cooling and lubrication; effects of overcooling

Must study Very likely · 93%Easy

Asked in 4 of 5 papers · 37 marks in total

Study these

  • Gas temperature ~2500 °C; ~30 % of fuel energy goes to coolant; keep wall ~180-200 °C
  • Without cooling: oil film breaks, piston seizure, thermal stress, knock/pre-ignition, low volumetric efficiency
  • Overcooling: poor vaporization, more heat loss, lower thermal efficiency, oil dilution and sludge, corrosion, more friction

Must study Very likely · 80%Easy

Asked in 4 of 5 papers · 76 marks in total

A2

Proximate vs ultimate analysis; HCV vs LCV

Must study Very likely · 93%Medium

Asked in 4 of 5 papers · 36 marks in total

Study these

  • Proximate: moisture (108-110 °C), volatile matter (950 °C, 7 min, covered), ash (725 °C, open), fixed carbon by difference
  • Ultimate: C, H, N, S, O and ash by weight; gives theoretical air, CV (Dulong) and flue gas composition
  • HCV counts latent heat of the water formed; LCV = HCV − 9H × 2442 kJ/kg (water leaves as vapour)

Alternative fuels: ethanol, biodiesel, methanol, hydrogen

Must study Possible · 40%Easy

Asked in 3 of 5 papers · 50 marks in total

Study these

  • Ethanol: high octane (~108), E10/E85, lower heating value, renewable; Biodiesel (FAME) by transesterification, higher cetane, lower CO/HC/soot, slightly more NOx
